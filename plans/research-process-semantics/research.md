# Make incomplete records earn their verdicts

The theory should be built as one small, finite **compatible-history core**, kept separate from the existing BHL code. A partial record denotes the set of complete modeled histories it could have come from. A claim is *entitled* under a profile only when the BHL warrant holds in **every** compatible history, and that set must be **non-empty**. Every other construct then becomes an instance of that core or a side condition on it: omission models, statistical validity under researcher degrees of freedom, revision transport and adequacy countermodels. This is the incomplete-database notion of *certain answers*, moved from tables to research traces ([Gheerbrant–Libkin–Sirangelo, TODS 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2691190.2691194); [PODS 2013 version, HAL](https://inria.hal.science/hal-00908404); [Libkin, AI 2016](https://cdn.aaai.org/ocs/7893/7893-36883-1-PB.pdf)). It is also the "thorough" semantics of partial Kripke structures, which answers "unknown" only when two completions disagree ([Bruns–Godefroid, CONCUR 2000](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/3-540-44618-4_14)). The repo already has the decisive witness: `record_does_not_determine_conclusion` in `lean/Lara/Examples/BHLPartialRecord.lean` shows that the latest-test projection does not determine the method conclusion. In the new core that theorem becomes a one-line statement, `verdict = unknown`. The statistical layer fits the same shape. A guarantee survives an incomplete ledger only through a **certified upper bound K̄** on the number of tests (e-Bonferroni, e-BH) or through **predictable e-processes**. Without such a bound the warrant has to be ⊤, meaning "no warrant" ([Wang & Ramdas, JRSS-B 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.02824v5); [Ramdas et al., Stat. Sci. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.01948v2)). Nothing found in the searched literature mechanizes the combination of history, statistical belief and argumentation acceptance. Two cautions apply. Several plausible-looking laws are false: stricter profiles do not always entitle fewer claims, and quarantine is not monotone once attacks exist. These need Lean counterexamples before anyone tries to prove the positive versions. Also, everything below labeled **[proposal]** is the research team's own construction, not a published result.

## The repo already fixes three design constraints

The survey of `/home/yfhe/ara/Lara/lean` settles some choices before any literature comes in. First, `HistoryCoverage` in `lean/Lara/BHL/LaraBridge.lean` has the single constructor `completeModeled`. It appears only in Lean BHL files, `docs/theory-bhl.md` and `docs/bhl-theorem-inventory.jsonl`, not in Haskell or on the wire. Extending it therefore touches no corpus. Adding a constructor would still break every exhaustive match on it and change the documented inventory. **[proposal]** The safer route leaves `HistoryCoverage` alone. It adds a new `RecordCoverage` type in a new module, with an embedding `completeModeled ↦ complete`, and proves a conservativity theorem: under `complete`, the new verdict coincides with the existing `ArtifactWarrant`. This mirrors the Coq data-provenance work, whose adequacy theorem says that forgetting the annotation recovers the standard semantics ([Benzaken et al., CPP 2021](https://www.lri.fr/~keller/Documents-recherche/Publications/cpp21.pdf)).

Second, `History D := Multiset (D × TestId)` is **unordered**. That is already a coarsening in the adequacy sense. It cannot express "the analysis plan was committed before data access", the ordering predicate that the statistical notes treat as central. `World.trace` (a `List (State …)`) does keep order, and `Accessible` compares whole observation traces, which is synchronous perfect recall in the interpreted-systems sense ([Huang & van der Meyden, arXiv 1310.6423](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1310.6423)). **[proposal]** Ordering predicates must therefore be stated over the trace or over a new ordered ledger, never over `History`. A separating countermodel showing that `History` forgets order is one of the first adequacy theorems to write.

Third, the Lara core is Mathlib-free but **already mechanizes grounded semantics** (`Grounded.lean`: `Label` in/out/undec, `Status` justified/contested/defeated/gap). It also already has non-promotion theorems (`justified_nonpromotion` in `Blocked.lean`, `source_justified_nonpromotion`, `quarantine_nonpromotion_corollary`). The research notes say "no Lean 4 Dung mechanization exists". That is true of the public literature the researchers searched, but **false for this repository**. The new theory must import these statuses, not re-derive them. Note also that `justified_nonpromotion` carries a support-inclusion hypothesis and applies only to unblocked claims. That restriction is exactly what keeps it clear of the non-monotonicity traps listed below.

## A record denotes a set of histories, and verdicts quantify over it

### Certain answers give the template, and the existing witness is an opacity proof

Incomplete-database theory supplies the whole pattern. A record denotes a set of complete objects. Certain answers hold in all of them and possible answers in some. A representation system is *strong* when it is closed under the query language, and c-tables are strong for full relational algebra ([Duke lecture notes](https://www.cs.duke.edu/courses/fall15/compsci590.6/Lectures/Lecture-13.pdf); [Imieliński–Lipski 1984](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~david/tud/il84.pdf)). The key operational result is that **naive evaluation (computing on the record as if it were complete) is correct exactly when the query is monotone** in the information ordering the semantics induces; for most semantics that monotonicity is preservation under a matching class of homomorphisms ([Gheerbrant–Libkin–Sirangelo, TODS 2014](https://doi.org/10.1145/2691190.2691194)). Under the open-world assumption, a Boolean first-order query that naive evaluation answers correctly is equivalent to a union of conjunctive queries ([Sapienza seminar abstract](https://www.diag.uniroma1.it/node/5983)). Libkin also argues that intersection-style "certain objects" can give counter-intuitive results, and that "certain knowledge" (what is deducible about all answers) is often the better notion ([Libkin, AI 2016](https://cdn.aaai.org/ocs/7893/7893-36883-1-PB.pdf)). This is the argument for returning a per-claim *verdict* rather than a "best completed record".

Epistemic logic gives the same thing in different vocabulary. In interpreted systems, an agent knows φ when φ holds at every point it cannot distinguish from the actual one ([van der Meyden lecture notes](https://cgi.cse.unsw.edu.au/~meyden/3152/lecture4.pdf)). A secret predicate is *opaque* when every secret run is observationally equivalent to some non-secret run ([Bryans et al., opacity](https://www.informatik.uni-hamburg.de/TGI/pnbib/b/bryans_j1.html)). **[proposal]** The dictionary is: record ↔ the auditor's local state; compatible set ↔ the points the auditor considers possible; certain ↔ K; possible ↔ ¬K¬. Read that way, `record_does_not_determine_conclusion` proves the method conclusion is opaque under the latest-test projection. "φ is determined by obs" is a 2-safety hyperproperty in the sense of k-safety hyperproperties ([Clarkson–Schneider, J. Comput. Secur. 2010](https://doi.org/10.3233/JCS-2009-0393)). Its failure is witnessed by one finite pair of histories, which is why such failures can be checked with `decide` on finite spaces.

### Recommended core: four verdicts, a non-emptiness guard, and monotonicity in two directions

**[proposal]** The core is a structure `RecordSemantics H R` with `compat : R → H → Prop`, `[Fintype H]` and `[DecidableRel compat]`. The verdict type is `inconsistent | certainTrue | certainFalse | unknown`. The verdict is supervaluational: certainTrue only if every compatible history satisfies φ *and* the compatible set is non-empty. **Supervaluation** is the most precise sound semantics: it is supertrue at all precisifications, and it keeps the classical truths that **strong Kleene** evaluation loses, such as `λ → λ` for gappy λ ([Dopico, arXiv 2503.15007](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.15007v3)). Any compositional Kleene-style evaluator the checker actually runs should therefore be proved *sound against* the supervaluational reference (Kleene-true ⇒ super-true), with its incompleteness documented by a countermodel. The partial-model literature makes the same distinction between compositional 3-valued semantics and thorough semantics ([Bernasconi et al.](https://annabernasconi.faculty.polimi.it/wp-content/uploads/paper_30.pdf)).

The theorems to prove first are short and need no Mathlib heavy machinery:

| Theorem (proposed name) | Statement | Source of the idea |
|---|---|---|
| `verdict_sound` | `certainTrue → ∀ h, compat r h → φ h` | Certain answers |
| `positive_needs_nonempty` | `certainTrue ∨ certainFalse → ∃ h, compat r h` | Pitfall guard (see the last section) |
| `certain_refine` | if `[[r']] ⊆ [[r]]` then certainty transfers from r to r' | Libkin's semantics-induced order |
| `certain_weaken_assumption` | `compat₁ ≤ compat₂ → certain₂ → certain₁` | Omission-model ordering |
| `certain_factor` | `obs₁ = g ∘ obs₂ → K_{obs₁} φ → K_{obs₂} φ` | FHMV view hierarchy |
| `determined_iff_no_witness` | determinacy ↔ no pair with equal obs and different φ | 2-safety |
| `naive_sound_iff_factors` | a record-level evaluator is exact iff φ factors through obs | Naive evaluation |
| `partialRecord_unknown` | the existing witness restated as `verdict = unknown` | Existing `BHLPartialRecord` |

Mathlib's `GaloisConnection`, `GaloisInsertion` and `GaloisConnection.compose` ([Mathlib docs](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Order/GaloisConnection/Defs.html)) supply the abstraction stack history → full record → latest-test projection. This should be optional polish, not the foundation. The `Set H`/`Finset` version is enough for every theorem above. It also keeps proofs inside the axiom trio without `native_decide`.

### Omission models are compatibility relations, and only one is safe by default

Missing-data theory says when treating a record as if it were the data is valid. MAR (missingness independent of the missing values, given the observed ones) is required, and **MAR cannot be verified from the observed data** ([Heymans, ch. 7](https://bookdown.org/mwheymans/bookmi/missing-data-mechanisms.html); [Seaman et al., arXiv 1306.2812](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.2812)). Grünwald and Halpern show that naive conditioning in a space that ignores the observation protocol gives wrong answers (Monty Hall). Conditioning is correct only in the space that includes the protocol, and the condition that licenses naive conditioning (coarsening at random) "holds rather infrequently" ([arXiv cs/0306124](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0306124)). **[proposal]** So the compatible set must range over *(execution, reporting choice)* pairs, not over executions alone.

The ladder of compatibility relations is then: CWA (`obs h = r`), OWA (`r ≤ obs h`, which is literally the `Multiset` order `≤` on `History`), declared reporting policy (`∃ choice, P h choice ∧ report h choice = r`), and count-bounded OWA (OWA plus `card (obs h) ≤ K̄`). `RecordCoverage` has one constructor per rung. A profile names the weakest rung it is willing to assume. Under OWA, an adversarial reporter and a benign one give the *same* certain verdicts, because ∀ already covers the worst case. They differ only for possibility or probability readings.

Two different extensions of a partial history are easy to conflate. Completing the *unobserved past* differs from continuing into the *future*, because entitlement is non-monotone in future events: a later failed replication or retraction can defeat it ([adequacy notes on partial Kripke structures](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/3-540-44618-4_14)). **[proposal]** `compat` should describe only past completions. A separate `continues` relation should handle the future, and a countermodel should show that merging the two is unsound.

## Entitlement is profile-relative, non-factive and witness-keyed

### A justification norm with an explicit inductive-risk profile

Every norm of assertion in the literature shares one schema: "assert p only if p has C" ([SEP, Assertion](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/assertion/)). Truth and knowledge norms are factive, so a checker that sees only the history can never decide them. Justification norms (Douven, Lackey) are non-factive. They also handle Lackey's *selfless assertion* case, where a teacher properly asserts what the evidence supports without personally believing it ([SEP, Assertion](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/assertion/)). Context-sensitive variants (Gerken, McKinnon) let the required degree of support move with stakes ([SEP, Assertion](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/assertion/)). Douglas's inductive-risk argument says the evidential standard "necessary to warrant empirical assertion" involves weighing the costs of error, not evidence alone ([Inductive Risk preprint](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/25196/3/Inductive_Risk.pdf)). Pollock's warrant (support by an "ultimately undefeated argument" from the evidence basis) is already an operational justification norm ([SEP, Defeasible Reasoning](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/reasoning-defeasible/)). Mayo's severity requirement is relative to a particular error, so it fits as a per-error side condition ([Notes on Mayo's severity](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/311/1/severity.notes.html); [Mayo 2025](https://errorstatistics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/mayo-2025-encylopedia-article-.pdf)).

**[proposal]** A `Profile` is a declared inductive-risk policy. It has five fields: the admissible evidence kinds; the weakest acceptable `RecordCoverage` rung; the statistical guarantee class and level (FWER/FDR, α); the set of required error probes; and the acceptance status (Lara's grounded `justified`). The judgment is

`Entitled P r c :⇔ verdict_P r (fun h => Warranted P h c) = certainTrue`,

where `Warranted P h c` on a complete history is the existing `ArtifactWarrant` strengthened by `kinds ⊆ P.admissible`, `P.requiredErrors ⊆ probed`, and the statistical predicate of the next section. Truth stays separate. `Entitled` neither implies nor is implied by the claim, and the philosophical countermodels for each direction (unlucky assertion; Gettier) should become two-world Lean examples.

### No-promotion as a lattice-typing invariant over evidence kinds

The researchers found **no published "no-promotion" theorem for scientific claim kinds**. The nearest formal facts are non-collapse results in justification logic. J lacks factivity (`t:F → F`), so justified belief does not entail truth. J₀ with an empty constant specification justifies no axiom ([SEP, Justification Logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-justification/)). The sum axiom `E(s,X) ∪ E(t,X) ⊆ E(s+t,X)` is exactly the aggregation that a no-promotion law has to restrict ([SEP, Justification Logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-justification/)). **[proposal]** Define a finite evidence-kind lattice, roughly `hypothetical < conditional < supported < observed`, aligned with the existing `PayloadMode conditional | applied`. Require every rule to output a kind ≤ the meet of its input kinds. The no-promotion theorem then follows by structural induction on derivations: no derivation whose leaves are all ≤ `hypothetical` yields an `observed` claim. Pair it with four small countermodels. The first is supported ⊬ warranted: in an AF where b attacks a and b is unattacked, a is out. The second is conditional ⊬ applied: application needs both premises. The third is warranted ⊬ true. The fourth is hypothetical ⊬ supported. Mayo's error-relativity adds a second axis. A claim licensed against sampling error is not licensed against misspecification unless that error was probed, which reduces to a decidable `Finset` inclusion.

### Status equality is not warrant equality, and stricter is not smaller

Grounded labelling is nonmonotone. In h, argument a is in because nothing attacks it. In h′, b attacks a and c attacks b, so a is still in, but now only through c, and c may be quarantined under another profile. **[proposal]** Entitlement must therefore be keyed by (profile, history, justification witness), not by status. That mirrors justification logic, where `t:F` and `s:F` are different facts. The same nonmonotonicity breaks the natural law "stricter profile ⇒ fewer entitled claims". Removing an admissible kind can delete an attacker and reinstate a defeated claim. **[proposal]** Prove the law only under the condition that the stricter profile removes arguments that attack nothing in the target's ancestry. That case is covered by directionality: grounded semantics satisfies it, and stable semantics does not ([Baroni & Giacomin 2007](https://pietro-baroni.unibs.it/paperpages/RI-21.htm); restated in [arXiv 2507.06852](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.06852)). ASPIC+'s rationality postulates hold under grounded semantics even with unrestricted rebut ([Modgil & Prakken, arXiv 1804.06763](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06763); [Caminada COMMA'14](https://mysite.cs.cf.ac.uk/CaminadaM/publications/UnrestrictedRebutCOMMA14.pdf)). That is the argument for keeping entitlement skeptical, that is, grounded.

## Statistical validity becomes a ledger predicate with a fail-closed level

### What survives incompleteness, and what does not

Fixed-sample p-values need the sample size or stopping rule fixed in advance. Under data-dependent stopping, the error of "stop when p < α" inflates toward 1. The repeated-significance-testing result is Armitage, McPherson & Rowe 1969 ([J. R. Stat. Soc. A 132(2)](https://doi.org/10.2307/2343787)). Test supermartingales and e-processes avoid this: by **Ville's inequality**, `P(∃t: M_t ≥ 1/α) ≤ α` under *any* stopping rule, including an unrecorded one ([Ramdas, Grünwald, Vovk, Shafer 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.01948v2)). Optional continuation keeps type-I guarantees when e-values are multiplied ([Grünwald, de Heide, Koolen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.07801v4)), and a post-hoc choice of α is safe **exactly** for e-value-compatible rules ([Grünwald, PNAS 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.00901v3)). For multiplicity, **e-BH controls FDR ≤ K₀α/K under arbitrary dependence**, and any self-consistent e-testing procedure has FDR ≤ αK₀/K (Prop. 2) ([Wang & Ramdas 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.02824v5)). Running e-BH on a selected subset at α|S|/K stays valid for *any* selection, but still needs the full K ([Wang & Ramdas §5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.02824v5)). Gelman and Loken show that data-contingent analysis creates multiplicity even with no conscious fishing, so preregistering the hypothesis alone is not enough ([Gelman & Loken](http://www.stat.columbia.edu/~gelman/research/unpublished/p_hacking.pdf)). Selective inference targets error conditional on selection ([Fithian, Sun, Taylor](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.2597)). Online procedures (LORD, LOND, alpha-investing, e-LOND) set each level from past rejections and spending ([Javanmard & Montanari](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.06197); [Xu & Ramdas, e-LOND](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/xu24a.html)). Alpha-investing controls only mFDR unless further conditions hold ([Foster & Stine record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssb/v70y2008i2p429-444.html)). Under arbitrary dependence, the only admissible way to merge e-values is (weighted) averaging ([Vovk & Wang](https://ar5iv.org/html/1912.06116); [Wang, arXiv 2409.19888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19888)). A file-drawer-robust combination therefore has to average over *all* studies, unpublished ones included.

### Recommended predicates and the warranted-level function

**[proposal]** Each ledger entry gains five fields: an evidence-kind tag (p or e), a sequence index, a reference to the analysis commit, the set of datasets read during selection, and, for online procedures, the level used. The family gains a certified count bound K̄. The validity predicates are then:

| Predicate | What it certifies | What it requires of the record | Fails closed when |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Predictable` | each e-factor fixed before its data | choice index < first data access | any timestamp is missing; warrant falls back to the certified prefix product |
| `PlanCommittedBefore` | analysis σ(prior)-measurable | commit index < every access to D | any access lacks an index |
| `SplitSelection` | selective guarantee | selection reads only D₁, test only D₂, D₁ ⊥ D₂ | unknown provenance counts as touching D₂ |
| `CountBounded K̄` | Bonferroni, e-Bonferroni, e-BH at K̄ | a certified `card ≤ K̄`; unreported outcomes not needed | no bound means K̄ = ⊤ and no warrant |
| `LedgerComplete` | online FDR replay | every test, in order, with its outcome | any omission; a count bound **cannot** stand in for this |

The warranted level is a function `α' : Ledger → WithTop ℚ` that returns ⊤ whenever a required field is missing, so the fail-closed behavior is built into the type. For one reported e-value among m reported tests and at most u unreported ones, the level scales as α·(m+u)/m. That is exact in ℚ, monotone, and degenerates to ⊤ as u → ∞. The researchers derive three results themselves from Markov's inequality and the e-value definition; none is cited as a published theorem. **e-Bonferroni** ("reject iff e_i ≥ K̄/α") has FWER ≤ α under arbitrary dependence for any unreported set. Reporting the max of K e-values costs a factor K. **e-BH at K̄** inherits Prop. 2, provided unreported tests count as non-rejections. That last step needs checking, because Prop. 2 is stated for a fixed K.

These predicates connect to compatible histories in one theorem. **[proposal]** If `CountBounded K̄` is part of the coverage rung, then for every compatible completion the true family size is ≤ K̄, so the e-Bonferroni rejection is certainTrue-warranted. That is the positive counterpart to `record_does_not_determine_conclusion`. The current witness has two completions of sizes 1 and 2, and the verdict is unknown precisely *because* no bound is supplied. Given K̄ = 2 and the stricter threshold, it becomes determinate.

### Mechanization route: finite, rational and induction-based

At the pinned Mathlib rev 81a5d257, `OptionalStopping.lean` provides `Submartingale.expected_stoppedValue_mono` and `maximal_ineq`. `maximal_ineq` is Doob's inequality for nonnegative **sub**martingales, and a grep found **no Ville inequality** ([Mathlib OptionalStopping mirror](https://florisvandoorn.com/BonnAnalysis/docs/Mathlib/Probability/Martingale/OptionalStopping.html)). No proof-assistant formalization of p-values, e-values or multiple testing turned up ([Ramdas & Wang monograph](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.23614) is mathematics, not code). SampCert shows that Lean and Mathlib probability can carry production-grade DP proofs, but its PMFs are ENNReal, not rational ([arXiv 2412.01671](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.01671v2)). **[proposal]** Follow the existing `Tests/Binary.lean` style instead. Use a finite horizon N, a `Fintype` alphabet and rational null masses, and define `IsEValue` as a `Finset.sum` inequality. Prove finite-horizon Ville by induction on N, e-Bonferroni by Markov plus a union bound, e-BH by the self-consistency finite-sum argument, and the split-selection theorem by Fubini over a product `Fintype`. Each has a decidable counterexample twin: optional-stopping p-value inflation at a concrete N, and same-data selection over K analyses, where the realized record is identical in both worlds, which is the formal content of Gelman–Loken. Infinite-horizon Ville and online FDR should become GitHub issues with their costs stated, as CLAUDE.md requires, and stay out of the first milestone.

## Revision transports entitlement in three distinct ways

### Exact criteria exist for monotone support; only sufficient ones for attacks

The theories of change surveyed point to one finite object. The ATMS label of a node is its set of *minimal* assumption environments, and a node holds in E iff some label environment ⊆ E ([Edinburgh KRE tutorial](https://www.inf.ed.ac.uk/teaching/courses/kre/tutorials/tut9.pdf); [de Kleer 1986](https://doi.org/10.1016/0004-3702(86)90081-0)). Why-provenance is the same object in database form, the image of provenance polynomials N[X] under a homomorphism. For positive queries, every semiring homomorphism commutes with evaluation, and deleting tokens D is the homomorphism x ↦ 0 for x ∈ D ([Green, Karvounarakis, Tannen, PODS 2007](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf)). Finding a *minimal* deletion set is NP-hard for some query classes ([Buneman, Khanna, Tan, PODS 2002](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~sanjeev/papers/pods02_view_deletion.pdf)). For bases, Hansson's kernel contraction ([J. Symb. Log. 1994](https://doi.org/10.2307/2275912)) puts the matching necessary condition into words: "core-retainment" says an element β is removed only if some subset A′ of the base fails to imply the withdrawn α while A′ ∪ {β} implies it, so β lies in some α-kernel (a minimal α-implying subset). The Isabelle AFP mechanizes AGM over Tarskian consequence ([AFP Belief Revision](https://www.isa-afp.org/entries/Belief_Revision.html)). AGM ([Alchourrón–Gärdenfors–Makinson 1985](https://doi.org/10.2307/2274239)), however, works on closed belief sets with no record of sources, which makes it the wrong layer for quarantine.

**[proposal]** On the monotone (support-only) fragment, give Lara an **iff**: after quarantining Q, claim c remains warranted ⇔ some environment in `label c` is disjoint from Q. This sharpens the current sufficient conditions behind `tighten_artifact_warrant_loss`. Once attacks are present, the criterion is only sufficient, because quarantine can delete a defeater and *reinstate* a claim. That is pitfall #2 below.

For the attack-bearing fragment, argumentation dynamics gives locality. Under grounded semantics, edits outside a claim's ancestry leave its status unchanged (directionality) ([Baroni & Giacomin](https://pietro-baroni.unibs.it/paperpages/RI-21.htm)). Splitting and division-based recomputation give the exact new status of the affected region ([Baumann–Brewka–Wong](https://www.springerprofessional.de/en/splitting-argumentation-frameworks-an-empirical-evaluation/3860100)). Robustness principles classify which edits persist ([Rienstra et al., A&C 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3233/AAC-200520)). Enforcement, the problem of forcing a desired outcome, is impossible in general under restricted expansions ([Coste-Marquis et al., IJCAI 2015](https://www.cril.univ-artois.fr/%7Ekonieczny/papers/ijcai15a.pdf)). Strong equivalence ("same extensions under every expansion") is characterized by syntactic identity of semantics-specific kernels ([Oikarinen–Woltran, AIJ 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2011.06.003)). Lara should *check* proposed re-establishments and quarantine sets, never search for minimal ones.

### Renaming is a satisfaction condition, and injectivity matters only for counting

Institutions formalize "truth is invariant under change of notation" through the satisfaction condition `M' ⊨ σ(φ) ⇔ Mod(σ)(M') ⊨ φ` ([nLab](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/institution); [Goguen–Burstall copy](https://courses.grainger.illinois.edu/cs522/sp2016/InstitutionsAbstractModelTheory.pdf)). **[proposal]** `rebind_warrant` is a satisfaction-condition instance. Warrant transports along *every* renaming iff the checker never compares identifiers. Injectivity is necessary precisely where the semantics counts distinct sources: corroboration, independence, freshness. The provenance view makes this concrete. A non-injective merge sends x·y to x², which keeps bare positive warrant but destroys independence counts ([GKT 2007](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~green/papers/pods07.pdf)). The upgrade from sufficient to characterizing conditions is a pair of theorems: naturality of `Warranted` under arbitrary maps for the count-free fragment, plus a two-source merge countermodel for any count-sensitive profile (for instance, e-value averaging over K studies).

Build systems give the reuse rule. A stored result may be reused when the hashes in its *verifying trace* (everything it actually read) are unchanged, and early cutoff stops propagation when a recomputed value is unchanged ([Build Systems à la Carte, JFP 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956796820000088), §2.3 early cutoff, §3.6 correctness, §5.2 verifying traces). **[proposal]** Lara's content-addressed `encodeAtomKey` makes early cutoff nearly free. The theorem is "if the trace records all reads and all read values are equal, then `Warranted_new c = Warranted_old c`". It follows from the checker being a deterministic function of its read set, and it is sound **only if dependency recording is complete**. The resulting three-way classification is:

| Class | Criterion | Exactness |
|---|---|---|
| Survives by structure | label disjoint from Q / outside ancestry / unchanged verifying trace | iff on monotone support; sufficient with attacks |
| Survives by transport | the change is a morphism under which `Warranted` is natural (rename, re-encode, equal-value cutoff) | iff relative to the morphism class |
| Must be re-established | reads the change non-trivially | checked by recomputing the affected region; minimal repair is NP-hard |

Katsuno–Mendelzon's split between update and revision ([Katsuno–Mendelzon, "On the difference between updating a knowledge base and revising it"](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511526664.007); [Boutilier summary](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~cebly/Papers/boutilier_cscsi94.pdf)) separates two kinds of edit. A dataset version change is an *update*: old warrants stay valid for the old version, indexed by version. A discovered bug is a *revision*: dependent warrants are withdrawn. Darwiche–Pearl iterated revision ([AIJ 1997](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0004-3702(96)00038-0)) becomes relevant only if Lara ever keeps an entrenchment order over sources.

## Adequacy means every modeled distinction carries weight

Four adequacy shapes stack. **LF adequacy** is a compositional bijection with canonical forms ([Harper & Licata, JFP](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-functional-programming/article/mechanizing-metatheory-in-a-logical-framework/E4EC2F77CEF3DC56EABCF6F6658B179E); [Twelf wiki](https://twelf.org/wiki/adequacy/)). **Computational adequacy** makes the model agree with observation. **Full abstraction** makes model equality coincide with contextual indistinguishability, and Plotkin's PCF result is the canonical case of an adequate but not fully abstract model ([Plotkin 1977](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(77)90044-5)). **Felleisen-style separation** exhibits phrases that are equivalent under a coarser translation yet distinguished by a context ([Felleisen 1991](https://www2.ccs.neu.edu/racket/pubs/scp91-felleisen.pdf)). Hennessy–Milner (logical equivalence = bisimilarity, for image-finite systems) is the template for "the logic distinguishes exactly the representation's distinctions" ([Hennessy–Milner, JACM 1985](https://doi.org/10.1145/2455.2460)). BHL itself is sound and relatively complete with respect to a Kripke model ([Kawamoto, Sato, Suenaga, arXiv 2208.07074](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2208.07074)), so an HM-style invariance/separation pair over `Accessible` is in reach.

**[proposal]** The adequacy package for this theory has four parts. (A1) Encoding: a section/retraction pair between decoded Lara artifacts and the Lean history and ledger types. (A2) Conservativity: under `RecordCoverage.complete`, the new verdict equals the existing `ArtifactWarrant`. (A3) Invariance: entitlement respects the representation's equivalence (`Quotient.lift`). (A4) Separation, which is the priority: for every candidate coarsening π, a theorem `∃ h₁ h₂, π h₁ = π h₂ ∧ Entitled h₁ c ∧ ¬Entitled h₂ c`, proved by `decide` on tiny instances. The coarsenings to separate are:

| Coarsening π | Countermodel content | Status |
|---|---|---|
| latest-test projection | unreported earlier test flips the conclusion | **exists**: `record_does_not_determine_conclusion` |
| `History` as `Multiset` (forget order) | plan committed before vs after data access | to write; motivates the ordered ledger |
| forget failed or unreported analyses | same-data selection over K analyses | to write |
| forget selection-read provenance | split vs same-data selection | to write |
| collapse source identities (non-injective rebind) | corroboration count halves | to write |
| forget revocation/retraction | quarantined defeater reinstates | to write |
| PROV graph of the process | identical PROV, different entitlement | to write; strongest external positioning |

The PROV row matters for positioning. PROV-SEM gives PROV a first-order semantics with soundness but not full completeness ([W3C prov-sem](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-sem/); [Cheney WG email](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-prov-wg/2013Apr/0055.html)). PROV records what happened, and **absence of a record is not a record of absence**. A Lean witness of two processes with identical PROV graphs but different entitlement would be a citable separation result.

### Pitfalls and false conjectures to refute in Lean before proving anything positive

| # | Tempting conjecture | Why it fails | Lean action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A stricter profile entitles fewer claims | grounded labels are nonmonotone; removing an attacker reinstates | countermodel; prove only the restricted directional version |
| 2 | Quarantine only removes warrant | quarantining a defeater's support reinstates its target | countermodel; iff only on the monotone fragment |
| 3 | Naive evaluation on the record is sound | sound iff φ factors through obs or is monotone; the BHL conclusion does not factor | `naive_sound_iff_factors` plus the existing witness |
| 4 | Replaying online FDR from the ledger is safe | omitted non-rejections inflate replayed wealth, which is anti-conservative | countermodel; require `LedgerComplete`, not a count bound |
| 5 | An empty compatible set is harmless | supervaluation makes everything vacuously certain | `inconsistent` verdict; `positive_needs_nonempty` |
| 6 | Unchanged status means unchanged warrant | the basis can switch to a different defender | witness-keyed entitlement; countermodel |
| 7 | Completing the past = continuing the future | entitlement is non-monotone in future events | separate `compat` and `continues`; countermodel |

| 9 | Ordering predicates work on `History` | `Multiset` forgets order | separation countermodel; use the trace or an ordered ledger |

Two process constraints apply to every row. Every theorem, including each countermodel, gets a `#print axioms` row in `lean/AxCheck.lean`. And because `decide` over `Fintype` history spaces gets expensive, instances must stay tiny, with no `native_decide`. A change that touches `lean/` must pass `make local-gates`, and the PR should say so.

## Conclusion

The literature changes what the "incomplete history" problem is about. It is not mainly a statistics problem or an argumentation problem. It is a **representation problem with a known shape**. Every guarantee that survives incompleteness does so because some quantity is *bounded* or *predictable*: a certified K̄, an e-process fixed before its data, a label environment disjoint from the quarantine set, or a verifying trace with complete reads. Every guarantee that fails does so because it needs the *completion itself*: online wealth replay, MAR, or exact family size. That gives the formalization one organizing rule. A profile may assume only a coverage rung whose required quantity the record certifies. Everything else defaults to `unknown` or ⊤.

The recommended order follows from that rule and from where the risk sits. First, write the separating countermodels and the false-conjecture refutations, which are cheap, decidable and protect against proving the wrong laws. Next comes the Mathlib-side compatible-history core with `RecordCoverage` and its conservativity theorem. After that come the finite-rational statistical predicates, with e-Bonferroni first and e-BH second. Revision transport comes last, as the monotone-fragment iff plus naturality under renaming. None of this requires touching the Mathlib-free core, the wire or the corpus. The one real cost decision is when to replace the unordered `History` with an ordered ledger at the binding level, and that should be budgeted as its own issue before the statistical layer begins.
