## Arbitration

Per the plan's Global Constraints the **Lean executable semantics is the
oracle**. `differential.sh` is the enforcement point: any Haskell-vs-Lean
disagreement is a bug (in Haskell or in a fixture), fixed with a recorded note —
never a silent re-annotation of an expected verdict. No disagreements were found
in this closeout.

## Numeric-literal caveat (must persist)

The Haskell core normalizes numbers via `canonNum` (`Lara.Prop.nfTerm`); the
Lean driver runs at `canon = id` and does **not** canonicalize numbers
(documented in `lean/Lara/Driver.lean`). A unit containing a **non-canonical
numeric literal** can therefore make the two drivers diverge. Every corpus /
differential fixture avoids non-canonical numeric literals (the corpus uses
nullary and constructor atoms; no `num` terms), so the drivers stay
byte-identical. When adding fixtures: avoid numeric literals, or use only
already-canonical decimals. If a divergence ever appears, check for a numeric
literal before suspecting a real checker bug.

_Deviation from the plan's strongly-preferred Lean `dump`-mode:_ the corpus is
generated from real `Unit` values via the Haskell `encodeUnit` (not a Lean
emitter) and confirmed faithful by the both-drivers harness. Rationale: (1) no
existing Lean `Lara.Unit` carries 100+ args to dump; (2) the Lean `Lara.Unit`
stores no argument ids and has no `encodeUnit`, so a faithful Lean emitter would
be a non-trivial addition to proof-critical code; (3) `differential.sh` already
enforces the Lean-is-oracle arbitration byte-exactly, which is the guarantee the
dump-mode was meant to provide. The Lean driver was left untouched, so the
`check` contract, the two committed fixtures, and AxCheck are unaffected.

## Why R2 / R8 / R9 / R14 have no `checkUnit` golden (by design)

From `src/Lara/AST.hs` `RejectClass` haddock and spec §10.1:

- **R2 (signature well-formedness)**, **R8 (leaf admission)**, **R9
  (data-integrity / replay identity)** are **outside the executable acceptance
  core**. `checkUnit` consumes a decoded `Unit`; these classes concern
  environment/signature/admission facts that are not decided at the executable
  `checkUnit` boundary. They therefore have no `checkUnit`-level golden.
- **R14 (codec)** is the **decode-boundary** class: a malformed wire input is
  rejected by `decodeUnit`/`decodeUnitFile` before any unit exists to check. Its
  rejection matrix lives in `test/WireSpec.hs` (the malformed-input matrix:
  text-level errors, one-field-short/long per construct, unknown tags, bad
  naturals, and the two wire-well-formedness invariants), not in `CheckSpec`.

So the executable checker's rejection surface is exactly R1, R3, R4, R5, R6, R7,
R10, R11, R12, R13 plus the four structural outcomes — the classes with goldens.
